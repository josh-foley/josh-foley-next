Every few months I do a pass on this site and remove something. The
last thing to go was a "reading time" estimate. Before that, social
share buttons, a custom font, an animated header. None of them were
load-bearing.

The trend on personal sites is the opposite — more interaction, more
cleverness, more proof that you _can_ render a particle simulation in
the corner of your homepage. I've shipped my share of those. They
were fun to build and immediately invisible to the reader who just
wanted to know what I thought about React Server Components.

## What "boring" buys you

A boring blog has a few quiet advantages:

1. **Speed.** No JavaScript on the critical path means the first
   paint is roughly the time it takes to fetch HTML.
2. **Permanence.** Static HTML and a couple of CSS files will work
   in five years. A bespoke design system probably won't.
3. **Focus.** When the design has nothing to look at, the writing
   has to carry the page.

That last one is the real reason. If I'm honest, every animated
flourish I've ever added was a hedge against the writing being
boring on its own.

## The taste line

There's a difference between _minimal_ and _austere_. A blog that
strips every visual cue feels like a Word document. The trick is
keeping just enough — a confident type scale, a tiny bit of color,
some breathing room — so the page feels designed without feeling
designed _at_ you.

Whether I've hit that line on this site, I genuinely can't tell.
But I'd rather aim for it and miss than build another particle
simulation.
